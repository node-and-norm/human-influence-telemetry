## Completed synthetic demonstration

The generated result compares four synthetic Command records against a binary human-presence label. The label records the formal role only. A tested intervention controlled by the institution and a vendor-controlled mechanism both yield human presence, while their encoded HIT Command findings differ. Structural absence and unresolved authority also remain distinct in HIT. The demonstration shows representational distinctions supplied by the rules. It cannot establish that users benefit from them or assign them correctly from documents.

## Question and fair comparators

Can reviewers produce more defensible, actionable documentary assessments using HIT within a reasonable effort budget?

Use three conditions: a binary human-presence checklist; a competent structured documentary review asking about actors, timing, authority, contradictory evidence, uncertainty, and next evidence requests; and HIT 0.4.0. The structured comparator prevents an advantage obtained solely by comparing a detailed method to an impoverished checklist. Give every condition the same sources and task, with equivalent training time and no expected answers.

## Candidate design

Target six eligible reviewers, each reviewing three different packets, with condition and packet balanced using a Latin-square assignment repeated twice. No reviewer sees a packet twice. Packets must cover usable authority, constrained authority, and uncertain evidence, chosen without knowing participant outputs. This is a feasibility pilot; six reviewers and three packets do not support population-wide benefit claims. Record reviewer background and method familiarity.

Before recruitment, freeze packet contents, hashes, inclusion rules, condition instructions, time limits, assignment table, completion definition, and analyst access. Freeze a rubric for evaluating unsupported conclusions and evidence-request specificity. Reference judgments need a rationale and adjudication record; author judgments are not independent truth. Any panel disagreement stays visible. If a panel is unavailable, report descriptive task outputs and defer accuracy claims.

Primary observations: unsupported material conclusions per assessment, recorded against the frozen reference; and actionable evidence requests, defined as naming a missing proposition plus an identifiable artifact or record holder. Also record completion time, clarification requests, unresolved propositions, abandoned tasks, and confidence before and after source review. Avoid a composite quality score. A request count alone rewards verbosity, so report supported requests and redundant requests separately.

Report each case and condition descriptively, with original outputs. Preserve dependence within reviewers and cases; do not treat dimensions as independent participants. The candidate is too small to estimate general effects reliably. A later powered study needs an explicit sampling population and variance assumptions. Report recruitment failures and withdrawals; never remove poor-performing packets after seeing results.

## Stop and amendment rules

End a task at the predeclared time limit or participant withdrawal; retain partial outputs where consent permits. Close the pilot at the registered recruitment deadline even if incomplete. Any design change receives a new version before affected outputs are inspected. Negative or inconclusive results are publishable outcomes.

## Decision use

Observed ambiguity should inform handbook examples or a versioned contract proposal. Excessive burden should inform shorter workflows. A useful pilot result could support a bounded usability claim; it cannot establish reduced institutional harm, legal conformity, or successful current-contract replication.
