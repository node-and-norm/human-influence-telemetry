# Closest-method challenge to HIT's contribution

Claim-evidence links and audit documents already belong to established assurance methods. HIT must explain what its specific representation contributes without presenting those general practices as new.

## Review boundary

This targeted extension was conducted on 3 October 2026. Queries were `site.arxiv.org assurance cases AI safety claims evidence arguments framework` and `site.assurancecase.org GSN standard goal evidence context`. Search results nominated sources; only the primary sources below support the comparison. Raji et al. was followed from the existing review. No database export, exhaustive screening count, saturation finding, or full standards review is claimed.

The [ICO's Annexe 5](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/explaining-decisions-made-with-artificial-intelligence/annexe-5-argument-based-assurance-cases/), introductory paragraphs and Main components, describes contextual claims linked through arguments to evidence and subordinate claims. It also describes evidence repositories. Its page carries an under-review notice; this comparison uses its conceptual explanation and draws no legal-conformity conclusion.

[Raji et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.00973v1), §§4.1–4.3 and 4.6, includes stakeholder mapping, documentary records, risk analysis, and reflection leading to mitigation plans. Section 4.2.1 stresses perspectives beyond an individual developer. HIT's solo study cannot reproduce that diversity through multiple model calls.

[Safety Cases: How to Justify the Safety of Advanced AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10462), abstract only, organizes evidence-backed safety arguments. The abstract establishes a relevant comparison candidate; full-text evaluation of its argument structures remains pending.

## Operational comparison

| Question | Closest-method evidence | Consequence for HIT |
|---|---|---|
| Is linking claims, supporting claims, and evidence new? | ICO's Claims, Arguments, and Evidence sections already describe those relations. | Do not claim novelty for the graph structure or dependency idea alone. |
| Is documenting actors and audit stages new? | Raji's Mapping Stage identifies stakeholders and their involvement; the framework organizes audit artifacts. | Actor fields and audit documents alone cannot establish novelty. |
| Does an evidence-backed argument establish practical human control? | These sources describe argument structures and audit processes with broader purposes. | HIT still needs a justified mapping from documents to its six dimensions. A well-formed record cannot supply that justification by itself. |
| Does HIT outperform a capable review? | The three partial development dossiers propose ties. | Restrict the current contribution to the implemented representation and checks. Comparative benefit remains unresolved. |

These comparisons identify overlaps. They do not establish that the adjacent methods lack HIT's exact features; that negative claim would require a wider and deeper search. A feature checklist alone would also miss differences in purpose, implementation, and user effort.

## Candidate contribution after the challenge

HIT provides a versioned representation and executable checks for documentary judgments about practical human authority. The repository exposes the finding rules, missing-evidence state, actor and period boundaries, and claim-publication controls so that researchers can inspect and contest them. The development audits show why source-level objections must remain visible alongside valid software outputs. Neither unique novelty nor superior assessment quality has been established.

A stronger comparative result would require a task on which HIT prevents a consequential error or reduces a measured burden beyond a capable baseline. A tie is informative: it narrows the contribution to standardization and inspectability, whose practical benefits still require evaluation. Full review of assurance-case implementations, contestability methods, and the six-dimension construct remains open.
