## Why the boundary changes

HIT-SOLO-001-B examined two announcements dated 17 August 2020. That packet could establish an announced decision but contained no later implementation record. This extension asks whether additional public primary records support a complete current-contract assessment of the regulatory intervention and its reported downstream execution. It preserves the earlier dossier unchanged.

The assistant has seen the earlier dossier, its proposed ties, and search snippets reporting later grade issuance. This is an exploratory enlargement after prior results. It is neither preregistered confirmatory research nor a held-out case. Broad knowledge of the reversal and possible model training exposure are disclosed. No particular score, demonstrated benefit, or successful completion is required.

## Assessment boundary

The assessed institutional unit is Ofqual's governing authority for the event that changed the summer 2020 GCSE, AS and A level grading rule in England on 17 August 2020. The primary event period is 17 August 2020. The declared follow-up period is 18 to 20 August 2020, covering the reported issue of revised AS/A level results and GCSE results. Later publications may provide retrospective evidence about that event and follow-up, but later institutional reforms fall outside the assessment.

The assessment separates Ofqual, the Secretary of State/Department for Education, exam boards, education centres, and affected candidates. An exam board's action is not attributed to Ofqual without evidence of direction or a stated authority relationship. The unit is an event-level regulatory intervention, not an individual student's appeal, the normal practice of each awarding body, or all of Ofqual's 2020 conduct.

The system role is statistical standardisation of teacher-proposed grades. Inclusion tests HIT's automation boundary; no machine-learning status or general validation across AI systems is claimed. The rubric remains specification/schema/handbook 0.4.0, with no new categories or semantic amendments.

## Common questions and matched outputs

Both the HIT record and the capable structured baseline receive the same packet and answer these questions:

1. Which named authority took the decision and controlled its implementation route?
2. What does the record establish about that authority's access and reasoning before the decision?
3. What substantive reconsideration, altered grading rule, and downstream execution are established for the event?
4. Does the packet establish qualifying harm, responsibility for a remedy, and any delivered or operationally directed remedy? Which person-level consequences remain unknown?
5. Which source dependencies, counterevidence, missing records, and alternative interpretations could change those conclusions?
6. What proportionate evidence request or next action follows, including no additional action where justified?

The capable baseline records actor, period, proposition, source locator, counterevidence, uncertainty and next evidence request. It may express every distinction the evidence supports. Both outputs are assistant-authored proposals; shared authorship, order effects and prior rubric knowledge prevent an independent or causal comparison. Report added distinction, tie, loss or unresolved with a reason. Neither speed nor user benefit will be estimated.
