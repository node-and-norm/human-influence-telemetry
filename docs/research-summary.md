# Human Influence Telemetry: findings, use and next steps

Reviewed 29 October 2026. This account synthesizes the linked records at source revision `6e2aabcd07912c7c5df4b9a20c069745db87923d`. It adds no validation claim, release decision or change to the underlying method.

Status: Maintenance. Evidence: One preserved human exercise.

## Question and contribution

What documentary evidence shows that human judgment retained practical influence, and can reviewers assess it consistently?

A documentary assessment method and a preserved exercise in which two scorers agreed on seven of seven items in one case packet under the earlier scoring contract.

## Findings to date

Two eligible independent scorers reached seven of seven exact agreements and zero critical disagreements on one frozen Cigna PxDx packet under contract 0.1.0. Both used the same substantive rating category.

This narrow exercise passed its predeclared advancement gate. The lack of category variance leaves Cohen’s kappa undefined; exact agreement here does not establish general reliability or current-contract replication.

## How to use the work

Organize evidence of human authority, identify missing support and compare recorded judgments with their stated basis.

## Limits and next evidence

Current-contract replication, the independent human implementation audit, broader validity and stable-release gates remain unresolved. Software checks and AI-assisted development do not supply independent human validation.

Reopening development requires a named problem, evidence requirement, owner and stopping condition. Stable release and a new empirical study retain their separate gates.

## Research origins

Human Influence Telemetry emerged from research for *Poenitentia Institutionum: Prudence, Responsibility, and Institutional Repentance in the Age of Artificial Intelligence*, a forthcoming chapter in *AI from a Catholic Perspective*. The inquiry into prudence and institutional responsibility raised a practical question: what evidence would show that human judgment retained meaningful influence over a decision? HIT developed as a method for examining that question through documentary records.
